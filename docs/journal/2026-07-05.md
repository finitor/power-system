# 2026-07-05

## Spurious charge-disable relay pulse — a dropped BMS charge-enable frame

Tim observed relay CH2 (Classic AUX2 charge-disable) briefly energize at
~06:25 local — SOC low (~74%), PV near nil. A charge-disable at dawn with a
half-empty bank is exactly backwards, so it looked like an allocator bug.

**It wasn't the allocator's arithmetic.** The allocation event at
`2026-07-05T10:25:27Z` recorded `reason: "BMS charge disabled"`,
`charge_ceiling_a: 0.0`, both controllers `disable:true` — and the very next
cycle (10 s later) returned to `unconstrained`. A single-cycle blip.

The trigger chain:

- `charge_enabled` is read from the Pylon `0x35C` request-flags frame
  (`request_flags.charge_enable`). If that frame is missing from a read,
  `request_flags` is `None`.
- The old code defaulted `charge_enabled = not self.live` (i.e. **False** in
  live mode) and only lifted it to True when `request_flags is not None` —
  "fail conservative: unreadable charge-enable ⇒ treat as disabled."
- `None` ⇒ `charge_enabled=False` ⇒ `ChargeCeiling` returns
  `(0.0, "BMS charge disabled")` ⇒ allocator zeroes both targets ⇒ relay CH2
  fires. `"BMS charge disabled"` is on the immediate/deadband-bypass list, so
  nothing debounced it.

**Genuine BMS stop vs dropped frame — the record separates them cleanly.**
A real `charge_enable=0` is stored as a `charge_enable=0.0` sample; a missing
frame stores nothing. The recorder shows `charge_enable=1.0` at 10:24:55 and
again at 10:25:58 — high on both sides, ~30 s each way — and the 10:25:27
decision still carried `bms_ccl_a:200.0` and `battery_current_a:-2.9`, so the
`0x351`/`0x356` frames arrived that cycle; only `0x35C` was absent. Across the
whole store the two populations are unmistakable:

| Period | "BMS charge disabled" events/day | `charge_enable=0` samples | Max SOC | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 21–29 | 60–85 | 265–390/day | **100%** | Genuine — pack hit full, BMS legitimately cut charge |
| Jul 3–5 | 4–7 | **0** | 95–97% | Spurious — no genuine drop possible; pack never full |

The 06:25 event is in the July population: zero genuine `charge_enable=0`
samples, pack at 74%.

**The bus is healthy — this is not CAN ill-health.** `0x35C` broadcasts at
~72 Hz; a 1.5 s read normally sweeps ~100 copies. It's absent from only ~1
read in 2000 (`voltage` 1977 vs `charge_enable` 1976 samples since Jul 4).
`can0` error counters are pristine: `ERROR-ACTIVE`, 0 bus-errors / arb-lost /
error-warn / bus-off / restarts, 33 RX errors across 1.2 B frames. The rare
miss is a benign gs_usb/USB delivery hiccup that assembles a partial snapshot
(`BatteryCanClient.read()` breaks on the first `recv()` gap) — invisible to
CAN-layer counters, not a degradation signal.

### The fix — `ChargeEnableResolver`

New resolver in [`charge_ceiling.py`](../../software/pi-controller/src/offgrid_power/charge_ceiling.py)
(commit `31a20fc`), documented in
[`charge-controller.md`](../subsystems/charge-controller.md#bms-charge-enable-fault-tolerance-missing-0x35c):

- **frame present** → use the bit verbatim (a genuine stop acts immediately);
- **absent < grace** (`CHARGE_ALLOC_ENABLE_HOLD_S`, 45 s) → hold last-known
  (debounce the dropped frame);
- **absent ≥ grace** (sustained blindness) → **release** to the controllers'
  own regulation rather than latch charge off.

Degraded (blind) transitions emit a `charge_enable_degraded` event + journald
line. Cold gate: a blind release is suppressed below
`CHARGE_ALLOC_COLD_RELEASE_BLOCK_C` (2.0 °C) using the **CAN-independent**
ambient sensor (GPIO 4), since with the BMS dark we can't see pack temp.

### Why *release* is the safe default, not *disable*

Tim's key push-back: disable-on-blindness is not conservative — sustained
"not charging" is the fatal wedge for an off-grid bank. The reasoning that
settled it:

- **Asymmetric cost.** Over-charge is bounded (controller voltage regulation +
  BMS hardware cutoffs cap the top). Under-charge from a latched stop is
  unbounded — it walks the bank to blackout and takes the supervisor and its
  watchdog down with it. Self-reinforcing.
- **Hardware already fails the other way.** Relay CH2 is fail-off (Javino
  S1/S2 HIGH): a fully-dead Pi de-energizes it → Classic charges. The old
  software asserting a *stop* on *partial* telemetry loss was thus *less* safe
  than a total Pi death.
- **The supervisor's disable is an overlay.** When it loses its inputs it
  should remove itself from the loop, not seize the actuator in a stop state.
- Also fixed an inconsistency: **total** CAN loss (`battery is None`) already
  released via the ceiling's "no battery telemetry" path; only **partial**
  loss disabled. Partial was treated more conservatively than total.

**Backstops verified before changing behavior** (live values):

- Classic absorb **57.0 V** / float 56.9 V, temp-comp capped at 57.0, EQ=57.0.
- EPEver boost/float **57.0 V**, hard clamp 60.0 V.
- Both self-limit at 57.0 V (3.56 V/cell) — **1.4 V below the BMS CVL of
  58.4 V**. Running both wide open cannot overcharge; 57.0 V is a safe
  indefinite resting voltage.
- BMS `_PROTECTION_FLAGS` include cell over-voltage **and** under-temperature
  cutoffs, acted on in hardware independent of the CAN request and the Pi.

16 new tests (13 resolver unit + 3 record-level integration incl. the exact
06:25 scenario). Given the ~0.05% drop rate, expect ~3–7 spurious pulses/day
eliminated — matching the observed Jul 3–5 counts.

## Is CANBUS in the reboot-escalation loop? (design question)

Tim asked whether high CAN error rates or outright CAN failure feed the
USB-reset → reboot escalation. Traced both watchdogs:

- **`can-watchdog`** (local): triggers *only* on outright bus **silence**
  (`rx_packets` frozen 10 s), then unbind/rebinds the USB-CAN adapter. No
  reboot, 10-min cooldown, never looks at error rate.
- **`supervisor-watchdog`** (reboot): reboots when **≥3 of 4** transports
  `{classic, epever, magnum, battery}` are `status ∉ {ok,disabled}` for
  ≥10 min. `battery` = CAN.

Findings:

- **High error *rate*: not involved.** `reader_error_rates` is diagnostic/
  display only; the health `checks` status is presence-based (read failed /
  no data), never rate-based. A CAN bus that errors but still delivers frames
  = `battery: ok` = contributes nothing to the quorum.
- **Outright CAN failure: involved, but only as 1 of the required 3.** CAN
  death alone can't reboot the Pi — it needs 2 more transports down at once,
  realistically a USB-bus-wide brownout taking epever+magnum+battery while
  Classic (Modbus-TCP/ethernet) survives (the 2026-06-30 shape). CAN
  participates; it can't drive the loop solo.
- **The blindness fix is decoupled from — and dampens — this loop.** A missing
  `0x35C` leaves `battery` non-`None` (the partial-read guard only trips on a
  missing `0x356` measurements frame), so "sustained blindness" reads as
  `battery: ok` and never touches the quorum. More importantly: the loop's
  root cause is a 5 V/USB **brownout** from rail sag; keeping charge *enabled*
  while blind holds the battery/5 V rail *up*, working against the brownout —
  whereas the old *disable*-on-blindness deepened the discharge that caused it.
  Net: the fix reduces reboot-loop risk.

## Dedicated BMS-protection alert channel

BMS self-protection flags (hardware cutoff engaged) already surface as
**ERROR** status conditions
(`battery_protection_status_condition_candidates`) and drive the
`/api/v1/health` verdict — that part predates today. What was missing: a BMS
hardware trip shared the email-only `supervisor-degraded` check with routine
"one controller offline" WARNINGs. Since a protection trip means the last line
of defense actually engaged (and in a normally supervised system must never
happen), it now has its own high-urgency channel (commit `8cceddb`):

- [`scripts/bms-protection-notify.sh`](../../scripts/bms-protection-notify.sh)
  — pings `HC_BMS_PROTECTION_URL /fail` the moment a `BMS protection:`
  condition is present (alarms/pre-trip WARNINGs stay on the degraded channel),
  recovery ping on clear. Unreachable/unparseable health is deliberately *not*
  treated as a trip.
- `offgrid-bms-protection-notify.{service,timer}` — every 1 min (a cutoff is
  urgent). Wired into `deploy.sh`; documented in
  [`healthchecks-escalation.md`](../runbooks/healthchecks-escalation.md).

Provisioned to a Healthchecks check (email for now, like the others) and
verified end-to-end: a synthetic protection payload sent the `/fail` ping and a
synthetic recovery sent the up ping, both accepted, state left clean.

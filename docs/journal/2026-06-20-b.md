# 2026-06-20-b — Top-balance experiment: 57.0 V scalar setpoint

## What and why

Moved both charge controllers' scalar setpoint to **57.0 V** (≈3.5625 V/cell on
16S), up from the ~56.x range, using the operator voltage nudge. The goal is two-
fold: see whether we can reach a higher SOC tomorrow without a cell running away,
and — the part that's already paying off — use the elevated voltage deliberately
as a **balancing tool**.

This is squarely within the [charge-mechanics](../charge-mechanics.md) reasoning:
near the top of the LiFePO4 knee the OCV curve is steep, so small per-cell SoC
differences show up as voltage differences the BMS can actually see and act on,
and passive balancing only bleeds the high cells above its threshold. At the
lower setpoints the pack tapered and relaxed before the cells spent meaningful
time in that window, so accumulated imbalance never got worked off. Holding 57.0
keeps the cells up in the balancing band long enough each day for the balancer to
catch up. The collapsed absorb/float means that window stays open (the controller
keeps offering the voltage instead of dropping to a lower float).

## Observations so far (afternoon/evening of the change)

- The upward setpoint creep has been uneventful — no runaway, no protection
  events.
- Cells are **better balanced than they have ever been**. The two chronic outlier
  cells in pack 2 — the ones that used to sit permanently as the min and max in
  the Cells telemetry line — are no longer showing up as the extremes.
- This looks self-reinforcing: tighter balance lowers the spread, so the highest
  cell sits lower for a given pack voltage, which is what made 57.0 safe rather
  than runaway in the first place.

## Notes for interpretation

- Judge the top of charge by **max-cell and delta**, not the 57.0 pack number —
  the average is 3.5625 V/cell but the top cell rides above it.
- Capacity returns above here are small; the real win is balance, not the last
  sliver of SOC. The endgame is to find the *lowest* setpoint that keeps the pack
  balanced and full enough, then back down to it once the drift is worked off and
  capture the calendar-life benefit. Re-check after a few days whether balance
  holds at a slightly lower hold.

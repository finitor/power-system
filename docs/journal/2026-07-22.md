# 2026-07-22

## Negative instantaneous-load regression from asynchronous telemetry

The terminal scrollback captured repeated impossible Load readings during
fast-changing solar input, including −16.6 A / −908 W at 12:47:34, −14.5 A /
−793 W at 12:48:00, and −10.9 A / −595 W at 12:49:29. The underlying Classic
and BMS readings were individually plausible. A durable example showed the
actual defect: the load sample at 12:38:22 combined Classic output of 16.2 A
captured at 12:38:18.189 with BMS charge current of 31.9 A captured at
12:38:22.440, a 4.25-second skew, and mechanically reported −15.7 A.

The June 9 per-device actor change made device I/O non-blocking, but each reader
scheduled its next poll from the previous poll's completion. The 1.5-second CAN
collection therefore drifted in phase relative to the fast Classic Modbus
read. Snapshot composition then took the latest cache entry from each reader
without checking their acquisition times. Rapid cloud-edge changes made the
latent mismatch visible. One-minute durable samples have caught negative loads
on many days since June 21, as low as −18.8 A; five-second displays exposed
many more.

Readers now timestamp acquisition start, stay on a fixed cadence, skip missed
slots rather than shifting phase, and retain eight successful values. Snapshot
composition selects the newest complete load cohort whose controller/BMS
timestamps are within 2.5 seconds and no more than two poll intervals old. A
brief missed poll can therefore reuse the previous complete cohort while the
next CAN collection finishes. If an enabled source remains unavailable past
the 10-second normal-cadence grace, instantaneous load becomes unavailable and
the allocator receives no feed-forward load allowance. It does not assume that
a communications-silent controller stopped charging, because that cannot be
distinguished from a controller continuing autonomously behind a failed data
path. The BMS feedback clamp remains the downstream protection.

## Snapshot failure was an undersized tmpfs, not a full disk

Creating a consistent telemetry snapshot at `/tmp/metrics-snapshot.sqlite`
failed with SQLite's `database or disk is full`. No persistent filesystem was
full: the Pi root had 51 GB free and the telemetry SSD had 451 GB free. `/tmp`
is a 453 MB RAM-backed tmpfs, while the live database is 2.7 GB (681,845
4 KiB pages). The database contains about 5.54 million scalar samples since
June 20, has no free-list pages, no runaway WAL, and no active fallback store;
the size is allocated live history plus indexes, not reclaimable debris.
Primary-store writes continued normally throughout the investigation.

The snapshot helper now preflights SQLite page size/count against destination
free space and reports the required and available capacity before opening the
output. Full snapshots belong under `/srv/telemetry/snapshots` on the SSD and
should be removed after analysis rather than accumulated. The live database
must still be opened only as `offgrid`; no cleanup, `VACUUM`, logging restart,
or tmpfs enlargement is needed.

All 524 tests pass after the telemetry and query-tool changes.
